## Abstract

This study investigates gain-saturated semiconductor optical amplifiers as a mechanism for mitigating atmospheric scintillation in free-space optical communication systems.

The work examines carrier dynamics, gain saturation, amplified spontaneous emission, and the trade-off between turbulence mitigation and signal-quality degradation.

A numerical model is developed to investigate the response of a semiconductor optical amplifier under turbulence-induced optical intensity fluctuations.

The results demonstrate the potential of gain-saturated SOAs to reduce scintillation while also introducing signal-quality penalties, highlighting an important trade-off for the design of reliable FSO communication links.

## Research Contribution

The study provides a numerical framework for investigating the interaction between semiconductor optical amplifier gain saturation and atmospheric turbulence.

The work forms part of a broader research programme on adaptive semiconductor optical amplification for reliable free-space optical communication.
