# Amplified Spontaneous Emission (ASE) Noise Model

## Overview

Amplified spontaneous emission (ASE) noise is one of the dominant noise sources in optical amplifier-based communication systems.

In this work, ASE noise is considered for both EDFA and SOA amplification models to evaluate the amplifier-induced signal degradation.

The ASE contribution directly influences:

- receiver SNR
- optical signal-to-noise ratio (OSNR)
- amplifier noise figure

---

# ASE Noise Power

The ASE noise power generated by an optical amplifier is expressed as:

\[
P_{ASE}=2n_{sp}(G-1)h\nu B_o
\]

where:

- \(P_{ASE}\) is ASE noise power.
- \(n_{sp}\) is spontaneous emission factor.
- \(G\) is amplifier gain.
- \(h\nu\) is photon energy.
- \(B_o\) is optical bandwidth.

The factor 2 represents two polarization modes.

---

# EDFA ASE Noise

For an EDFA:

\[
P_{ASE,EDFA}
=
2n_{sp,EDFA}
(G_{EDFA}-1)
h\nu B_o
\]

The low noise figure of EDFA results from efficient population inversion of erbium ions.

Typical values:

| Parameter | Value |
|-|-|
| Gain | 20–25 dB |
| Noise figure | 4–6 dB |
| \(n_{sp}\) | 1.2–1.5 |

---

# SOA ASE Noise

For an SOA:

\[
P_{ASE,SOA}
=
2n_{sp,SOA}
(G_{SOA}-1)
h\nu B_o
\]

The ASE contribution depends on:

- carrier density
- bias current
- gain saturation
- nonlinear carrier dynamics

---

# Role in SOA–EDFA Comparison

The amplifier noise penalty is expressed as:

\[
\Delta SNR_{NF}
=
10\log_{10}
\left(
\frac{NF_{SOA}}
{NF_{EDFA}}
\right)
\]

At weak turbulence:

\[
\Delta SNR_{NF}
>
\Delta SNR_{scint}
\]

therefore EDFA provides better performance.

At strong turbulence:

\[
\Delta SNR_{scint}
>
\Delta SNR_{NF}
\]

and SOA becomes advantageous.

---

# Summary

The ASE noise model provides the amplifier noise contribution required for the receiver SNR calculation.

Together with turbulence-induced scintillation, it defines the SOA–EDFA performance crossover condition.
