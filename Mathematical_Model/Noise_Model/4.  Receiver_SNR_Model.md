# Receiver SNR Model for Turbulent FSO Communication

## Overview

The receiver signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) determines the overall performance of the amplified free-space optical (FSO) communication system.

In this work, the receiver SNR is calculated by considering:

1. Amplifier gain contribution
2. Atmospheric turbulence-induced fading
3. Amplified spontaneous emission (ASE) noise
4. Shot noise
5. Thermal noise

The SNR model provides the performance metric used for SOA–EDFA comparison and crossover analysis.

---

# Received Optical Power

The received optical power after atmospheric propagation and amplification is expressed as:

\[
P_r=P_t h G
\]

where:

- \(P_r\) is received optical power.
- \(P_t\) is transmitted optical power.
- \(h\) is the Gamma-Gamma turbulence channel coefficient.
- \(G\) is amplifier gain.

The turbulence coefficient \(h\) introduces random power fluctuations caused by atmospheric scintillation.

---

# Photodetector Current

The optical signal is converted into electrical current:

\[
I_{pd}=RP_r
\]

where:

- \(R\) is photodetector responsivity.
- \(P_r\) is received optical power.

---

# Total Noise Contribution

The total receiver noise consists of:

\[
N_{total}
=
N_{ASE}
+
N_{shot}
+
N_{thermal}
\]

where:

- \(N_{ASE}\) is amplifier spontaneous emission noise.
- \(N_{shot}\) is photon shot noise.
- \(N_{thermal}\) is receiver thermal noise.

---

# Electrical Signal-to-Noise Ratio

The receiver SNR is calculated as:

\[
SNR=
\frac{I_{pd}^{2}}
{N_{total}}
\]

or in decibel form:

\[
SNR_{dB}
=
10\log_{10}(SNR)
\]

---

# Turbulence-Induced Performance Degradation

The atmospheric turbulence causes received power fluctuations:

\[
P_r=P_t hG
\]

where:

\[
h=X\,Y
\]

for the Gamma-Gamma channel model.

The turbulence contribution is evaluated through:

- SNR reduction
- power penalty
- scintillation index

---

# Power Penalty Calculation

The system power penalty is defined as:

\[
PP=
10\log_{10}
\left(
\frac{SNR_{reference}}
{SNR_{turbulent}}
\right)
\]

where:

- \(SNR_{reference}\) represents clear-channel performance.
- \(SNR_{turbulent}\) represents performance under atmospheric turbulence.

---

# SOA–EDFA Crossover Criterion

The amplifier selection depends on the competition between:

## Noise Figure Penalty

\[
\Delta SNR_{NF}
\]

caused by amplifier noise differences.

## Scintillation Suppression Benefit

\[
\Delta SNR_{scint}
\]

provided by SOA nonlinear carrier dynamics.

The crossover occurs when:

\[
\Delta SNR_{scint}
=
\Delta SNR_{NF}
\]

At this point:

\[
C_{n,crossover}^{2}
\]

defines the transition between EDFA-dominated and SOA-dominated operation.

---

# Simulation Outputs

The receiver SNR model provides:

- received power
- total noise power
- SNR versus turbulence strength
- power penalty
- SOA–EDFA crossover point

These outputs are used for numerical validation of the analytical model.

---

# Summary

The receiver SNR model integrates amplifier noise, atmospheric turbulence, and optical propagation effects into a unified performance evaluation framework.

This model forms the basis for the computational implementation used to reproduce the SOA–EDFA comparison results.
